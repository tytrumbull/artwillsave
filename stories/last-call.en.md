The bartender had already put the chairs up on the tables when the woman asked for another mezcal.

"We're closed, sweetheart."

"One. The last one. I swear on my mother, may she rest in peace as of four o'clock this afternoon."

He looked at her for a long time. Then he took a chair down, poured one for himself, and sat across the bar from her.

"What was her name?"

"Rosa. Fucking hell, what a corny name, right? My whole life I told her she sounded like a soap opera."

"My mom's name was Guadalupe."

"That's worse."

They laughed, which was the last thing either of them expected to do that night. They drank to Rosa and to Guadalupe, then to every mother with a corny name, and after that they didn't know what they were drinking to, but they kept drinking to it.

At three, he turned off the sign. She left a five-hundred-peso bill he wouldn't take, and it stayed on the bar anyway.

Outside, the street smelled like rain. She walked slowly, because for the first time all day there was no one she had to call.
